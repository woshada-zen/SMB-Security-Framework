## 1. Purpose

This Acceptable Use Policy defines appropriate and inappropriate use of [Company Name]'s information technology resources, including computers, networks, email, internet access, cloud services, and mobile devices. The policy aims to protect company assets, ensure productivity, and maintain legal compliance.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:
- All employees, contractors, temporary staff, and volunteers
- All company-owned and personal devices (BYOD) accessing company resources
- All IT resources including Microsoft 365, Azure, on-premises systems, and third-party applications
- All locations including offices, remote work, and travel

## 3. General Principles

Users of [Company Name] IT resources must:
- Use resources primarily for legitimate business purposes
- Protect company confidential information and intellectual property
- Respect copyrights, licenses, and intellectual property rights of others
- Maintain professional and ethical conduct in all communications
- Report security incidents and suspicious activity immediately

## 4. Acceptable Use

### 4.1 Email and Communications

**Permitted:**
- Business-related email communications
- Professional communication with clients, partners, and vendors
- Incidental personal use (limited, non-disruptive)
- Use of company email signature and branding

**Guidelines:**
- Apply appropriate sensitivity labels to emails (see Data Classification Policy)
- Do not send Confidential or higher data to personal email accounts
- Use encryption for emails containing sensitive information
- Avoid large attachments; use OneDrive/SharePoint sharing instead
- Include confidentiality disclaimers on external emails

### 4.2 Internet Access

**Permitted:**
- Business research and professional development
- Access to approved cloud services and SaaS applications
- Brief personal use during breaks (non-disruptive)

**Guidelines:**
- Internet activity may be monitored for security and compliance
- Use company VPN when on public Wi-Fi networks
- Report suspicious websites or phishing attempts

### 4.3 Microsoft 365 and Cloud Services

**Permitted:**
- OneDrive for Business for work files and collaboration
- SharePoint for team sites and document libraries
- Microsoft Teams for chat, meetings, and collaboration
- Approved third-party integrations (see Cloud Services Usage Policy)

**Guidelines:**
- Do not sync Confidential files to personal devices without encryption
- Use sensitivity labels on all documents
- Share files using secure sharing links (not public links)
- External sharing requires business justification and manager approval

### 4.4 Software and Applications

**Permitted:**
- Company-approved software listed in IT software catalog
- Browser extensions approved by IT Security
- Mobile apps from Company Portal (Intune-managed devices)

**Guidelines:**
- Do not install unauthorized software on company devices
- Request new software through IT ticketing system
- All software must be licensed and up-to-date
- Shadow IT (unapproved cloud services) is prohibited

### 4.5 Mobile Devices and Remote Work

**Permitted:**
- Use of company-issued mobile devices for business purposes
- Limited personal use of company devices
- BYOD enrollment in Intune for email access (with consent)

**Guidelines:**
- Keep devices locked with PIN/password (minimum 6 digits)
- Enable Find My Device / remote wipe capability
- Do not jailbreak or root devices
- Report lost or stolen devices within 1 hour
- See Mobile Device Policy and Remote Work Security Policy for details

## 5. Prohibited Activities

The following activities are **strictly prohibited**:

### 5.1 Security Violations

- Sharing passwords or using another person's credentials
- Attempting to bypass security controls (firewalls, DLP, authentication)
- Unauthorized access to systems, data, or networks
- Installing malware, hacking tools, or unauthorized software
- Disabling antivirus, endpoint protection, or security updates
- Connecting unauthorized devices to company network

### 5.2 Data Misuse

- Copying Confidential or Restricted data to personal devices/accounts
- Sharing company data with unauthorized third parties
- Using company data for personal gain or competitive purposes
- Downloading sensitive data without business justification
- Exfiltrating data to personal cloud storage (Dropbox, Google Drive, etc.)

### 5.3 Inappropriate Content

- Accessing, storing, or transmitting illegal content
- Viewing, downloading, or sharing pornographic material
- Engaging in harassment, discrimination, or hate speech
- Gambling or gaming (except company-approved team building)
- Cryptocurrency mining or trading

### 5.4 Network Abuse

- Launching denial-of-service attacks or network flooding
- Port scanning or network reconnaissance without authorization
- Unauthorized network sniffing or packet capture
- Creating or distributing viruses, worms, or malware
- Unauthorized wireless access points (rogue Wi-Fi)

### 5.5 Misrepresentation

- Impersonating other individuals in emails or communications
- Falsifying email headers or sender information
- Making unauthorized commitments on behalf of the company
- Posting as company representative on social media without authorization

### 5.6 Illegal Activity

- Copyright infringement (pirating software, music, videos)
- Insider trading using company resources
- Any activity violating local, national, or international laws
- Fraud, theft, or embezzlement

## 6. Personal Use Guidelines

Limited personal use of company IT resources is permitted provided:
- It does not interfere with job duties or productivity
- It does not consume significant bandwidth or storage
- It complies with all other policies
- It does not create legal or security risks for the company

**Examples of acceptable personal use:**
- Checking personal email during breaks
- Online banking during lunch
- Emergency personal calls
- Brief personal research

**Examples of unacceptable personal use:**
- Running a personal business using company resources
- Excessive personal phone calls or internet browsing
- Streaming music/video during work hours (except background during tasks)
- Personal projects consuming significant time or resources

## 7. Social Media and External Communications

### 7.1 Personal Social Media

- Personal social media use on company devices should be minimal
- Do not disclose confidential company information on social media
- Do not claim to represent the company unless authorized
- Respect colleagues' privacy (no posting photos without consent)
- Follow company Social Media Policy if separate policy exists

### 7.2 Professional Social Media (LinkedIn, Twitter)

- Maintain professional tone in posts associated with company
- Do not engage in public disputes with clients, competitors, or colleagues
- Clarify that personal opinions are your own, not company positions
- Report social engineering attempts or impersonation of colleagues

## 8. Monitoring and Privacy

### 8.1 Monitoring Rights

[Company Name] reserves the right to monitor:
- Email communications (subject to local privacy laws)
- Internet browsing activity
- Network traffic and bandwidth usage
- Files stored on company systems
- Microsoft 365 activity logs
- Mobile device management data

### 8.2 Privacy Expectations

- Users should have no expectation of privacy when using company IT resources
- Personal data on company systems may be accessed during investigations
- Monitoring conducted in compliance with employment laws and data protection regulations
- Personal devices enrolled in BYOD have limited monitoring (work apps only)

### 8.3 Legitimate Monitoring Purposes

Monitoring is conducted for:
- Security incident detection and response
- Compliance with legal and regulatory requirements
- Investigation of policy violations
- Network performance and capacity planning
- Prevention of data loss and intellectual property theft

## 9. Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)

Personal devices may access company email and data if:
- Device enrolled in Microsoft Intune MDM
- Device meets minimum security requirements (OS version, encryption, PIN)
- User consents to remote wipe of corporate data
- Company data segregated from personal data (work profile)

**BYOD Security Requirements:**
- Operating system must be current version or previous version
- Device encryption enabled
- Minimum 6-digit PIN/password
- No jailbreak/root
- Antivirus installed (if available for platform)

See Mobile Device Policy for complete requirements.

## 10. Data Retention and Deletion

- Company data remains company property, even on personal devices
- Upon termination, all company data must be returned or deleted
- Company may remotely wipe corporate data from BYOD devices
- Deleted emails may be recoverable from backups for retention period
- See retention schedules in Data Classification Policy

## 11. Reporting Requirements

### 11.1 Mandatory Reporting

Users must immediately report:
- Lost or stolen devices containing company data
- Suspected malware infections or security breaches
- Phishing emails or social engineering attempts
- Unauthorized access to systems or data
- Accidental data disclosure or loss
- Policy violations by other users

**Reporting Channels:**
- IT Help Desk: [phone/email]
- Security Incidents: [security@company email]
- Anonymous Hotline: [ethics reporting line]

### 11.2 Protection from Retaliation

- Good-faith reporting of security incidents is encouraged
- No retaliation for reporting suspected violations
- Prompt reporting may mitigate disciplinary consequences

## 12. Consequences of Violations

Violations of this Acceptable Use Policy may result in:

**First Offense (Minor):**
- Verbal or written warning
- Mandatory security awareness training
- Temporary suspension of privileges

**Repeat or Serious Offenses:**
- Suspension of IT access
- Suspension from employment
- Termination of employment or contract
- Legal action for criminal violations
- Reporting to law enforcement or regulatory authorities

**Factors Considered:**
- Intent (accidental vs. deliberate)
- Severity of impact
- History of prior violations
- Cooperation with investigation
- Self-reporting
