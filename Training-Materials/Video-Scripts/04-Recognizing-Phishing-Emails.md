# Video Script: Recognizing Phishing Emails

**Duration:** 6-8 minutes
**Target Audience:** All employees
**Learning Objectives:**
- Identify common phishing red flags
- Understand different types of phishing attacks
- Know how to report suspicious emails

---

## Script Outline

### [INTRO - 0:00-0:45]

**What is Phishing?**
- Fraudulent emails designed to steal credentials, money, or data
- #1 attack method (90% of successful breaches start with phishing)
- Getting more sophisticated—even experts get fooled

**Real Example:**
"Last year, one of our employees received this email..." [Show legitimate-looking Microsoft password expiration email]
"They clicked, entered their password on a fake page, and within 2 hours, attackers had accessed our financial system. Our quick response prevented £75,000 in fraudulent wire transfers."

**Visual:** Side-by-side of real vs. phishing Microsoft email

---

### [SECTION 1: Phishing Red Flags - 0:45-3:00]

**10 Red Flags to Watch For:**

**1. Suspicious Sender Address**
- **Look closely at email domain:** `ceo@compnay.com` (not company.com)
- **Hover over name to reveal address:** "Microsoft" <phishing@gmail.com>
- **Slightly misspelled domains:** micr0soft.com (zero instead of 'o')
- **Visual:** Zoom in on sender address showing subtle difference

**2. Generic Greetings**
- "Dear Customer" or "Dear User" (instead of your name)
- Legitimate companies use your name from their database

**3. Urgency or Threats**
- "Account will be closed in 24 hours!"
- "Immediate action required!"
- "Your password expires today!"
- Creating panic to bypass critical thinking

**4. Too Good to Be True**
- "You've won £5,000!"
- "Unexpected refund available"
- "Free gift card—click to claim"

**5. Suspicious Links**
- **Hover before clicking** (don't actually click)
- Link text says "microsoft.com" but URL shows "microsoft-login.phishing.com"
- Shortened URLs (bit.ly, tinyurl) hide real destination
- **Visual:** Hover demonstration showing mismatched URL

**6. Unexpected Attachments**
- Invoice you didn't order
- Resume from unknown applicant
- Shipping notification for unordered package
- File types: .zip, .exe, .js (often malware)

**7. Poor Grammar/Spelling**
- Professional companies proofread emails
- "Your account have been compromised"
- Awkward phrasing suggests non-native speaker or automated translation

**8. Requests for Sensitive Information**
- **Legitimate companies NEVER ask for passwords via email**
- Requests for SSN, credit card, banking details
- "Verify your account by replying with credentials"

**9. Mismatched Branding**
- Wrong logo, colors, or fonts
- Low-quality graphics (pixelated logos)
- Microsoft emails should look polished

**10. Unusual Requests from Known Contacts**
- CEO asking you to buy gift cards
- Colleague asking for password help via email
- Manager requesting urgent wire transfer with odd phrasing
- **Always verify through separate channel (phone call)**

**Visual:** Show 10 real phishing examples with red flags highlighted

---

### [SECTION 2: Types of Phishing Attacks - 3:00-4:30]

**1. Credential Phishing (Most Common)**
- Fake login pages mimicking Microsoft, banking, etc.
- Goal: Steal username and password
- **Example:** "Your OneDrive storage is full. Sign in to upgrade."
- **Visual:** Split screen showing real vs. fake Microsoft login page

**2. Business Email Compromise (BEC)**
- Impersonating executives or vendors
- Requesting wire transfers, gift cards, or W-2 forms
- **Example:** Email appearing from CFO: "Urgent acquisition. Wire £50k to this account. Keep confidential."
- **Red Flag:** Unusual request, external pressure for secrecy, urgency

**3. Spear Phishing**
- Targeted at specific individuals (you)
- Uses personal information to seem legitimate
- **Example:** "Hi [Your Name], I saw your LinkedIn post about [specific project]. Can you review this proposal?" [malicious attachment]

**4. Vishing (Voice Phishing)**
- Phone calls pretending to be IT support, bank, or government
- "This is Microsoft support. We detected a virus. Please install this tool..."
- **Red Flag:** Unsolicited tech support calls are ALWAYS scams

**5. Smishing (SMS Phishing)**
- Text messages with links
- "Your package delivery failed. Reschedule: [malicious link]"
- "Account locked. Verify now: [link]"

**6. Clone Phishing**
- Re-sending legitimate email you received before, but with malicious link
- "Resending this proposal from last week [now contains malware]"

**Visual:** Show real examples of each type (sanitized)

### [SECTION 3: What To Do - 4:30-6:00]

**If You Receive Suspicious Email:**

**STOP - Don't click anything**
- Don't click links
- Don't download attachments
- Don't reply (confirms your email is active)

**THINK - Check for red flags**
- Review sender address carefully
- Hover over links (don't click)
- Ask: "Was I expecting this?"
- Does it make sense?

**ACT - Report it**

**Option 1: Use "Report Phishing" Button** (Preferred)
- In Outlook, select email
- Click "Report Message" > "Phishing"
- Email automatically quarantined and reported to IT Security
- **Visual:** Screen recording showing how to report

**Option 2: Forward to IT Security**
- If button not available: Forward to security@company.com
- Include: When received, what made you suspicious
- Don't delete—we may need to investigate

**If You Already Clicked:**

**YOU CLICKED A LINK:**
- Don't panic—happens to everyone
- **If you entered credentials (username/password):**
  - Call IT Security immediately: [phone number]
  - We'll reset your password and check for unauthorized access
- **If you only clicked link (didn't enter info):**
  - Still report it, but lower urgency

**YOU DOWNLOADED ATTACHMENT:**
- Don't open the file
- Disconnect from Wi-Fi/ethernet immediately
- Call IT Security: [phone number]
- We'll scan your device for malware

**YOU SENT MONEY/DATA:**
- Contact IT Security and your manager immediately
- For wire transfers: Contact bank to attempt reversal (time-sensitive)
- For data exposure: We'll activate incident response procedures

**Visual:** Flowchart showing decision tree

---

### [SECTION 4: When in Doubt - 6:00-6:45]

**Verify Through Separate Channel:**

**Email claims to be from colleague/manager:**
- Call them directly (don't reply to email)
- Use phone number from company directory (not from email signature)
- "Hey, did you just send me an email about [X]?"

**Email claims to be from vendor/partner:**
- Call vendor using number from contract or website (not email)
- "I received an invoice. Can you confirm this is legitimate?"

**Email claims to be from Microsoft/IT:**
- Don't call number in email
- Contact IT Help Desk: [company phone number]
- "I received this email claiming to be from Microsoft. Is it real?"

**Remember:**
- **Legitimate companies won't mind you verifying**
- 30-second phone call can save thousands
- IT Security would rather get 10 false alarms than miss 1 real attack

**Visual:** Employee calling to verify, with satisfied expression

---

### [SECTION 5: Practice Makes Perfect - 6:45-7:30]

**Quarterly Phishing Simulations:**

**What to Expect:**
- IT Security will send simulated phishing emails quarterly
- These are TESTS (not real attacks)
- Help you practice identifying phishing in safe environment

**If You Click:**
- Redirected to training page (not actual malware)
- Immediate micro-training (5-minute review)
- No disciplinary action for first-time click
- We track to identify who needs extra training

**If You Report:**
- Congratulations—you passed!
- Helps us measure effectiveness
- Contributes to company-wide security culture

**Repeat Clickers:**
- If you click 3+ simulations: Remedial training required
- One-on-one session with IT Security
- Not punishment—we genuinely want to help you improve

**Visual:** Before/after metrics showing improvement (80% click rate → 15% after training)

---

### [CLOSING - 7:30-8:00]

**Key Takeaways:**
- Phishing is #1 attack method—you WILL encounter it
- Look for 10 red flags (sender, urgency, links, attachments)
- When in doubt, verify through separate channel
- Use "Report Phishing" button in Outlook
- If you clicked, report immediately (sooner = better)

**Action Items:**
- Save IT Security number in phone: [phone number]
- Practice reporting: Use "Report Phishing" button on next suspicious email
- Be patient with yourself—even experts get fooled occasionally
- Help colleagues: Share this knowledge with your team

**Support:**
- IT Help Desk: [phone/email]
- Security Team: [security@company email]
- Quick Reference: Phishing Red Flags Checklist (download from intranet)
